# CS 255: System Analysis and Design

## DriverPass Project

### Project Overview

DriverPass is a driver-training company that wanted to improve how students prepare for their DMV driving tests. The client requested a web-based system that would provide online courses, practice exams, and scheduling for in-person driving lessons. The system also needed to manage customer information, training packages, employee responsibilities, and appointment reservations. The primary goal was to create an accessible platform that would improve the customer experience while allowing DriverPass employees to manage daily operations efficiently.

### Strengths and Accomplishments

One aspect of the project I performed particularly well was identifying and organizing the client's functional and nonfunctional requirements. I was able to translate the information provided during the client interview into specific system requirements, including appointment scheduling, user authentication, role-based access, and customer progress tracking. I also developed UML diagrams to illustrate how different users would interact with the system and how its processes would operate. These documents helped establish a clear connection between the client's expectations and the proposed system design.

### Areas for Improvement

If I could revise one part of my work, I would focus on improving the system's UML diagrams by including additional details about exceptional situations and alternative processes. For example, the activity diagram for scheduling driving lessons could better illustrate how the system responds when an instructor becomes unavailable or a reservation conflicts with an existing appointment. Adding these details would make the diagrams more comprehensive and provide developers with clearer guidance when implementing the system.

### Understanding User Needs

I interpreted the user's needs by carefully reviewing the DriverPass interview transcript and identifying the responsibilities and expectations of each type of user. Customers needed a convenient way to access training materials and schedule lessons, while employees required tools to manage appointments, monitor progress, and maintain customer records. I incorporated these requirements into the business requirements document and represented the interactions through UML diagrams.

Considering user needs is essential because a system must support the people who will actually use it. Even a technically functional application can be unsuccessful if it does not address the client's business objectives or provide an accessible experience. Understanding these requirements early in development also reduces the likelihood of unnecessary revisions and additional development costs.

### Software Design Approach and Future Strategies

My approach to software design begins with understanding the problem, identifying stakeholders, and gathering functional and nonfunctional requirements. Once the requirements are established, I organize them into specific system functions and use diagrams to visualize how components and users interact. This process helps identify potential design issues before development begins.

In future projects, I would continue using client interviews, requirements analysis, and UML modeling to guide system development. I would also incorporate additional stakeholder feedback and review the system design throughout the development process. Regular communication with the client and iterative improvements would help ensure that the final product remains aligned with business objectives and user expectations.

The business requirements document showcases my understanding of requirements gathering, while the system design document demonstrates my ability to represent system functionality through UML diagrams. Together, these artifacts provide examples of my skills in systems analysis, software design, and problem-solving that I can present to potential employers.
